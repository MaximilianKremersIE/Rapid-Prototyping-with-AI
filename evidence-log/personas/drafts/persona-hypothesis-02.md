# Persona Hypothesis: The Prepared Insider

**Status:** Provisional Persona hypothesis requiring real-user validation

## Shared Job to Be Done

When one month before university starts with limited time to search for housing, approaching course start dates prompt international students to seek to secure housing matching their criteria and arrive at university already settled and stable, rather than starting the year still anxious and searching.

## Behavioral Archetype

International student with native or fluent Spanish language skills, higher budget (€2,000+), strong mentor guidance from an upperclassman who completed the search recently, and early start (early July). Searches strategically and systematically, calling landlords directly rather than relying on platform messaging. Feels confident in communication and negotiation. Still faces market scarcity (not enough options in first-choice neighborhood matching all criteria), but manages the constraint consciously by accepting a trade-off (Nuevos Ministerios instead of Salamanca) rather than panic-settling to a far-worse neighborhood. Maintains higher confidence in final choice even though it wasn't the original preference. Would pay for a curated platform that reduces anxiety about missing the optimal option.

## Decision-Relevant Profile

- **Relevant context:** International student with linguistic and financial advantages, mentored by older student who recently navigated the search, arriving in late August/early September with time to be selective.
- **Most relevant current behavior:** Starts early (early July) with systematic approach (5–6 hrs/week initially), calls landlords directly in fluent Spanish, receives guidance from mentor on neighborhoods and strategy, signs early (August 12th) with time margin before arrival, makes conscious trade-off between first-choice neighborhood and acceptable alternative when scarcity prevents getting both.
- **Current approach or alternative:** Uses Idealista + direct landlord calls (not just messaging), leverages mentor guidance for neighborhood recommendations and pricing context, compares options consciously, accepts compromise on one dimension (neighborhood) while securing quality on others (apartment condition, responsive landlord, manageable price within flexibility).
- **Decision-relevant constraints:** Market scarcity (limited options in Salamanca matching her criteria in her timeline); remote verification anxiety (even with advantages, feels uncertain about signing based on photos/video); dependence on mentor guidance (wouldn't have navigated as easily without Daniela's recommendations).
- **Product or research decision affected:** High-budget, resourced students like this persona may be **willing to pay for a curated platform** not to avoid panic, but to maximize confidence that they're making the optimal choice among available options. Product could have a premium tier targeting students willing to pay for curation, verification, and peace of mind.

# Persona Hypothesis: The Deadline Panic Settler

**Status:** Provisional Persona hypothesis requiring real-user validation

## Shared Job to Be Done

When one month before university starts with limited time to search for housing, approaching course start dates prompt international students to seek to secure housing matching their criteria and arrive at university already settled and stable, rather than starting the year still anxious and searching.

## Behavioral Archetype

International student facing their first housing search in Madrid, budget-constrained (€600–€900/month), with basic-to-functional Spanish language skills. Procrastinates on housing search due to underestimating how long the process takes and overestimating housing availability. When deadline pressure hits (1–2 weeks before arrival), frantically searches Idealista as their only known platform. Encounters ghosting, scams, and non-responsive landlords. When only one apartment responds and is available, settles for worse neighborhood, lower quality, or both rather than miss their arrival date. Even after signing, experiences low confidence in the choice and anxiety about having made a rushed decision.

## Decision-Relevant Profile

- **Relevant context:** First-time international student relocating to Madrid for university, arriving in late August/early September, relying on social network (upperclassman) for platform and strategy guidance.
- **Most relevant current behavior:** Procrastinates until 4–6 weeks before arrival, then escalates search effort 3–5x (from 2–5 hrs/week to 15–25 hrs/week), grinding a single platform (Idealista) with basic filters, messaging or calling landlords with low response rates, facing deadline-driven choice where "good enough" is the only option.
- **Current approach or alternative:** Uses Idealista exclusively (unaware of alternatives or trusts peer recommendation), relies on weekly browsing + recent-post sorting, messages/calls landlords, accepts first available apartment when deadline nears.
- **Decision-relevant constraints:** Budget constraint (€600–€900/month limits available options in desirable neighborhoods); language barrier (basic Spanish makes communication difficult, scam detection hard); timeline underestimation (thought more time existed than actually did); market scarcity (limited actual availability of options matching price + location + quality criteria in desirable zones in their arrival window).
- **Product or research decision affected:** Product must focus on **rapid discovery of options actually matching their criteria** (neighborhood, price, size, quality), not just search speed. The bottleneck is market scarcity, not search inefficiency. Without access to 6–7 real, verified options matching criteria, they will panic-settle.

## Validation Gaps

- **Would providing 6–7 curated options matching their criteria actually prevent panic-settling, or would they settle anyway?** (Confirmed as hypothesis through interviews, not yet tested with working prototype.)
- **What is the minimum time buffer needed before arrival date for them to comfortably choose without panic?** (Appears to be 3–4 weeks, but not confirmed.)
- **Would these students use a platform that costs money if it reliably showed matching options?** (Unknown; likely low willingness to pay given budget constraints, but not validated.)
- **Do all incoming cohorts follow this pattern, or are there subgroups with different behaviors?** (These 5 are representative of 15, but validation needed.)
