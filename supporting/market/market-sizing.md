# Market sizing

How many international students a year arrive at IE University Madrid and need to rent privately. Replaces the previous unsourced figure "~9,570" in [opportunity.md](../../evidence-log/opportunity.md#target-user). Tests the Area 3 assumption "target universities have enough international students" ([assumptions.md](../../evidence-log/assumptions.md#area-3-vendor--market-viability)).

## Result

| Measure | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| New bachelor's students at IE, foreign nationality, both campuses, 2024-25 | **1,731** | SOURCED [M2] |
| …of whom on the Madrid campus | **~1,300 to ~1,370** | PROVISIONAL SCENARIOS: depends on an unconfirmed Segovia split |
| New master's students at IE, foreign nationality | Unresolved | No published per-university master's intake |
| **International students needing a private rental in Madrid each year** | **Unresolved** | Needs master's intake, the Segovia split, and the share living with family or in residences |

**Previous figure.** opportunity.md gave "~9,570 target students", described as about 10,000 new students plus 1,000 transfers, minus ~13% local students, with no source. We could not find where it came from. Ministry data does not support "10,000 new students": IE had 2,139 new bachelor's students in 2024-25 [M2]. Total official enrolment that year was 9,939 (7,658 bachelor's + 2,281 master's) [M3][M4], but we have no evidence the old figure was based on it.

## Definitions

- **New student (new entrant):** first enrolment in a degree at that university that year ("estudiantes de nuevo ingreso" [M2]). Not the same as **total enrolment**, which counts every year of study. The two are never added together here.
- **International:** **foreign nationality** in the Ministry data. Not the same as living abroad: some foreign nationals already live in Spain, and some Spaniards live abroad. Not adjusted.
- **Official degrees only.** IE's non-official programmes, exchange and visiting students, and summer school are not in the Ministry data.

## Funnel

### Sourced steps (bachelor's, IE, 2024-25)

Latest published Ministry data, academic year 2024-25 (files created December 2025).

| Step | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A. New bachelor's students at IE | 2,139 | [M2] |
| B. Minus Spanish nationals (408) | **1,731** foreign (81%) | [M2] |

Trend: new IE bachelor's students rose from 1,627 (2021-22) to 2,139 (2024-25) [M2].

### Provisional step: remove the Segovia campus

IE describes Segovia as an undergraduate campus [M5]. **No source gives the number of new students by campus.** The only Segovia figure we found is total enrolment: a December 2025 news article reports more than 1,600 students on the Segovia campus, 90% of them foreign [M6]. IE had 5,770 foreign bachelor's students across all years in 2024-25 (7,658 total minus 1,888 Spanish) [M3].

Both scenarios **assume new students split between campuses the same way as all enrolled students**. That assumption is not confirmed.

| Scenario | Segovia share of foreign bachelor's students | New foreign bachelor's students, Madrid campus |
|---|---|---|
| A: the 90% foreign figure holds | 1,600 × 90% ÷ 5,770 = 25% | 1,731 × 75% = **1,298** |
| B: Segovia has IE's average foreign share | 1,600 ÷ 7,658 = 21% | 1,731 × 79% = **1,367** |

The Segovia count is from 2025-26 and the IE totals from 2024-25, so the shares are approximate. TODO (team): ask IE Admissions for new students by campus and nationality.

### Unresolved: master's students

The Ministry publishes master's **enrolment** per university, not master's new entrants. In 2024-25 IE had 2,281 official master's students, 1,633 of them foreign; 60% of IE master's students were full-time in 2023-24 (2024-25 not published) [M4]. That count covers every student enrolled that year, so we do not add it to new bachelor's students. Converting it to annual intake would need programme lengths we have not sourced.

TODO (team): ask IE Admissions for new master's students by campus, nationality and format (full-time, part-time, blended).

### Unresolved: students who won't need a private rental

Students living with family and students in residences must come out of the total. These are separate living arrangements, so subtract both as shares of the same total rather than one after the other.

| Step | What we found | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Living with family in Madrid | No source for IE or for foreign-nationality students in Madrid | TODO (team): add "Who do you live with?" to the [student survey](../research/student-survey.md); ask IE Student Services (ieustudentservices@ie.edu) whether they hold this data |
| Living in a student residence | No IE figure. Context only: purpose-built beds equal **7.8%** of on-site students in Madrid (all students, not only international) [M7]. In a 2023 survey of exchange students, **2%** of those in Spain lived in university-run housing [M8]. IE recommends residences and says they fill up quickly [M5] | TODO (team): ask IE Student Services how many new students book a residence through Beyond Campus or IE-recommended residences |

## Limits of this sizing

- Nationality is a proxy for "moving to Madrid to study"; it overstates the market at universities with many resident foreign nationals.
- IE's non-official programmes and exchange students are outside the Ministry data. TODO (team): ask IE Admissions for new intake by campus, programme type and country of residence.
- The Madrid-campus figures rest on one news article and an unconfirmed assumption about how new students split between campuses.

> **So what**
> The only sourced annual figure is 1,731 new international bachelor's students a year at IE across both campuses. The Madrid-campus share (~1,300–1,370) is a provisional scenario, and how many need a private rental is unresolved. As a recruitment choice, pilot with new bachelor's students on the Madrid campus, the group closest to what we can count; its exact size remains provisional, and we quote no market size until the team has IE's intake by campus and the private-renter share; plan a second university early (Universidad Europea de Madrid and Complutense have the next-largest foreign intakes).
> Linked assumption: "target universities have enough international students" ([Area 3](../../evidence-log/assumptions.md#area-3-vendor--market-viability)).
